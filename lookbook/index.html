A lookbook

Lace & Luggage

By Temitope Israel Balogun · Ibadan, Nigeria

Lace & Luggage is a lookbook by Temitope Israel Balogun, Founder & Tech-Archivist in Ibadan, Nigeria: Yoruba native wear and street clothes, 19 looks photographed in Ibadan.

Copycats can only copy what you did yesterday; they can never predict what you will design tomorrow.
Temitope Israel Balogun, Ilefoastro · first published 29 October 2026
Download the PDF

Chapter One

Lace & Luggage

Ceremony and the street in one outfit

Lace is what we wear to celebrate. A bag and heavy shoes are what we carry through the street. This chapter is native wear: lace, printed sets and plain bright colours, with woven caps, chains and dark glasses.

Chapter Two

Loud Fonts, Quiet Realities

Big letters on the chest, a calm face above them

The shirt speaks first and I answer later. This chapter is graphic tees, colour blocks and lettering, worn with plain shorts, flat caps and slides.

Chapter Three

Heavy Indigo

Denim piled at the ankle, a small eye at the neck

Heavy cloth and soft colour. This chapter is denim in every wash, stacked at the ankle or cut raw at the edge, with plaid, rhinestone patterns and an evil-eye pendant that keeps coming back.

Notes and credits

Photography. The cover and Look 1 (yellow lace) were photographed by Tynemoore. Looks 6, 9, 10 and 11 were photographed by the author's wife. Looks 8, 15, 17 and 18 were taken by the author on a timer. The remaining photographs were taken by the author's brother, Samuel, and by friends.

Tailoring. The yellow lace buba and kembe was made by the author's father at Mayor Fashion Center.

Styling. Temitope Israel Balogun styled every look.

Places. All photographs were taken in Ibadan.

Garments. Prints, lettering and labels on clothing belong to their makers. Brand names in the captions are given by the author, and no brand has supplied, endorsed or approved this book.

About the author

Temitope Israel Balogun is a Founder & Tech-Archivist based in Ibadan, Nigeria. By day, he preserves history through digital genealogy and the mapping of Yoruba lineage. By night, his work moves from the digital cloud to the physical pavement under the creative name ilefoastro.

For Balogun, style is an active archive. His design approach treats textiles like data sets, balancing the celebratory history of traditional native lace with the weight of global street culture. Whether organizing data or organizing an outfit, his goal is the same: to keep the identity of the past while building toward the future.

He is the author of books on Yoruba lineage and digital genealogy.
