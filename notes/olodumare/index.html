Olódùmarè: who the name refers to in Yoruba belief

What sources say, and where they differ

Olódùmarè is the traditional name for the Supreme Being in Yoruba belief. This note sets out what sources say about the name and the being. It does not speak for any religion or for the priests who practise it.

The name and other names

Olódùmarè, Olórun

Olódùmarè is described as the traditional name of the Yoruba Supreme Being. Olórun is another name, and it became more common through Christian and Muslim use. Some sources treat the two as one being. Others say that in some belief systems the different names refer to different aspects of the same being.

What the name means

uncertain

The origin of the name is uncertain. The scholar Bolaji Idowu read it as odu plus mare: "olodu" for one who holds fullness and authority, and "mare" for stability and constancy. Another reading, taken from the Odù Ifá, is "the owner of the pot from which the rainbow shines". Olórun is linked to ọ̀run (heaven). This note does not choose between them.

What sources say Olódùmarè does

creator and ruler

Olódùmarè is described as the creator and ruler of the universe. In one creation account the world begins as water, and Olódùmarè sends the Òrìṣà, led by Ọbàtálá, to form solid land. In another, Ọbàtálá shapes human bodies from clay and Olódùmarè breathes life into them.

Where sources differ

worship

One source says Yoruba worship Olódùmarè through the Òrìṣà, with no shrines or images dedicated directly to Olódùmarè. The same source also notes that some people do worship directly. This note records both statements and leaves the question to practitioners and further study.
