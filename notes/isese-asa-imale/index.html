Ìṣẹ̀ṣe, Àṣà and Ìmàle: what each word means

Three Yoruba words that are often mixed up

People often use these three words as if they meant the same thing. They do not. Here is what each one means, what sources say, and where they disagree.

Ìṣẹ̀ṣe

tradition

The traditional Yoruba religion and way of life, including family values, oral literature and history. Another name for the religion is Ẹ̀sìn Àbáláyé, "the religion of the ancestors".

Ìṣẹ̀ṣe Day is marked every year on 20 August. Osun State was the first state to make it a public holiday. Lagos, Ogun and Oyo have officially recognised it since 2023.

Àṣà

culture, custom

How people greet, dress, name children, marry and celebrate. A person can follow Yoruba àṣà whatever their religion. This is the difference from Ìṣẹ̀ṣe, which points to ancestral origin and indigenous spirituality.

Ìmàle

a Muslim; Islam

The Yoruba word for a Muslim, as in ọmọ Ìmàle. Some Yoruba Muslims prefer Mùsùlùmí (Muslim) and Ìsìláàmù (Islam).

Where the word comes from is debated. One view traces it to Mali, whose traders and scholars brought Islam to Yorubaland. Other views point to the Hausa word mālàmī or the Arabic mu'allim, both meaning "teacher". This note does not choose between them.

Do not confuse Ìmàle with Ìmọ́lẹ̀ (light) or Irúnmọlẹ̀ (primordial deities). They look alike when tone marks are left out.
